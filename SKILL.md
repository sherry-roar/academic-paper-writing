# Academic Paper Writing

Write for reviewers around the paper's strongest supported contribution. Treat the paper as a launch presentation: a purposeful argument that makes the contribution memorable. Adapt the terminology and format to the discipline, article type, author intent, and actual target venue.

Read [Argument and narrative](references/argument-and-narrative.md) for every substantive writing task. Its launch-presentation principles govern narrative choices throughout this skill.

## Establish the task

Use the supplied context to identify the requested output, allowed edit scope, manuscript stage, audience, and available evidence. Distinguish language polishing from structural revision and from new scientific work. Ask only for missing information that would materially change the result; proceed with useful independent work when possible.

For ordinary authorized edits, work directly. Use checkpoints when the user requests a collaborative process or when an unresolved scientific choice changes the intended claim. A request to polish prose does not authorize changing results, algorithms, equations, experimental settings, or the research question.

## Essential boundaries

- Preserve factual content, numerical precision, units, terminology, equations, and scientific meaning. Flag contradictions rather than silently selecting a convenient version.
- Never invent results, citations, quotations, author information, novelty, reviewer opinions, or venue rules. Mark unavailable evidence in the working draft or issue list.
- Match claim strength to the study design. Separate observation, interpretation, causal evidence, prediction, and proof.
- Keep factual records separate from manuscript narrative. Preserve numbers, units, comparison conditions, algorithm names, and evidence; organize the narrative around the strongest advantage. Explain less favorable comparisons through supported design objectives or trade-offs. Do not add unsolicited weakness summaries, defensive caveats, or a requirement to list negative results in the manuscript.
- Treat style examples as rhetorical guidance, not sources of scientific facts or text to copy. Accuracy takes priority over stylistic imitation.
- Preserve the user's authorization boundaries. Writing work does not authorize experiments, changes to scientific code, external uploads, contact with others, or submission.

## Work from argument to expression

1. **Recover the argument.** Identify the question, relevant prior knowledge, unresolved need, central insight, contribution, and supporting evidence. For language-only edits, use this internally to preserve meaning.
2. **Check the logic.** Align the abstract, introduction, methods, evidence, and conclusion. Each substantive paragraph should advance a recognizable part of the argument. Repair transitions by explaining relationships, not by adding stock connectors.
3. **Revise at the authorized level.** Organize around the research argument, then improve paragraphs and sentences. Preserve effective existing prose; change what improves accuracy, reasoning, clarity, or consistency.
4. **Verify the edited artifact.** Check facts against supplied sources, cross-section terminology and values, citations, figure references, and markup. Verify venue requirements only against current official instructions when that work is requested or relevant.
5. **Deliver the result.** Return or save the requested text and summarize material changes. Keep unresolved evidence, author decisions, and verification status in the accompanying working notes; do not automatically append them as caveats to manuscript prose.
